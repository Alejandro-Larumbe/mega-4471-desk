Three problems, one ticket.

The desk's table counts the one-day moves in the parser row. They are a different fault: a month/day swap moves a date by months, a one-day move moves it by one day. One fix does not cover both, and a month/day fix will not move a date that is one day out.

The three problems

parser — the shared parser reads every date month first. A day-first tenant writes the day first, so the month and the day swap. This is the fault #4471 was opened for, and the one the queued fix addresses.

hint — through the booking-v3 form, the tenant types what the printed hint tells them, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. The dates still land on the wrong day, but no shared-parser change touches them: the form’s hint is what is wrong here.

one-day-move — these read as a one-day move, not a swap. The desk caught them by hand and wrote them down; the tenant rule cannot see them, because it only knows the tenant, the form and the channel.

Two counts that do not match

The desk’s table and the reports disagree about how many reports each problem covers. Both numbers are in the export; only one of them is a count of the fault.

  • The export holds 427 rows for 412 reports: the ticket system exports in overlapping pages, and one report can come twice. One id, one report.
  • meta.intakeTag is what intake guessed from the reporter’s browser, at the moment the report came in. It is a guess, and it disagrees with the cause it is filed under here. The tenant, the booking form and the channel decide the cause; the tag does not.

So the counts in the table above are counted from the reports themselves, one id each, by the cause the tenant’s setup gives them.
